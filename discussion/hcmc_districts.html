Reading these

The districts are gone as administrative units. The July 2025 restructure replaced them with wards, so each familiar name here is the set of wards that now covers it. The ward names are listed beside each view; if a grouping does not match what you would call that district, it is worth correcting, because the grouping is mine and not official.

What to look for. Footprints should show the plot-and-alley grain, not blocks. Height should pick out the towers along the river and leave the rowhouse fabric flat. Roads should read as a network rather than a smear. Place density should concentrate on the market and the commercial streets. If any layer looks like noise where you know the ground is structured, that is a data problem worth finding before training rather than after.

Resolution. These are drawn at about 2.2 m per unit, the tier the pipeline notes call the first size that respects footprints, where a 3 m alley stays open and a 5 m rowhouse keeps its outline. The full 1.17 m unit would quadruple the file for detail a screen cannot show. The 9 m figure from the cost table is the cheapest tier, 128 px per tile, where a row of houses and its alleys fuse into one block.

Buildings and places from Overture; heights from 3D-GloBFP; roads, transit, water and green from OpenStreetMap; vegetation from WorldCover; population from WorldPop. Reproduce: scripts/render_districts.py
