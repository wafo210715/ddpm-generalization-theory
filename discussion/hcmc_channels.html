Distributions, per unit

Pooled over sampled tiles, counting every pixel. Axes are log1p so the zero spike and the tail are visible together. None of these is Gaussian and none needs to be. The reference papers' Gaussian mixtures are solvable toy models, not an assumption about data. What the shape decides is the transform each channel gets at B11, and the thing to watch is the zero fraction, because no monotone transform spreads an atom: a channel that is 86 % zeros stays 86 % zeros whichever transform you pick.

The same channels, correlated two ways

Spearman · left: between tiles · right: between units

This is the C1 baseline, the correlationist control, run on our own data. It matters more than it looks. Aggregate the channels to one number per tile and they collapse into each other — building coverage, floor-area ratio, road density and place density sit between 0.92 and 0.98, which would make four of six channels near-duplicates and is roughly the regression a driving-factor study runs. Look inside the tile and they come apart. Buildings against roads is −0.08 at unit level, effectively independent. The structure the model is given is the one on the right, and the tile-level view destroys it.

Does 128 px lose the city?

one dense tile, building channel, four resolutions

The open question about training cost. The answer is that the amount survives and the grain does not. Coverage is identical to three decimal places across all four, so any tile-level statistic is safe at 128. But edge density falls by a factor of nearly five, because at 9.4 m per pixel a tube house is smaller than one pixel and every pixel straddles building and alley. At 1.17 m a building spans several pixels and has an interior. If the claim is about morphology, 128 px is not a cheaper version of the same experiment.

What gets compared before and after training

You asked to be reminded. The comparison is one function run on both sides: the same descriptor measured on real cells now, and on generated cells at every checkpoint.

Before — this page
  • B10 per-channel marginals, zero fraction, skew, tails. Chooses each channel's transform. Gaussianity not required.
  • B10b the joint pattern of the six channels together, kept as a distribution so it can be compared later.
  • B11 transform, then standardize each channel and store the parameters, so a sample can be unpacked.
  • C1 the correlationist baseline, and the flag for near-duplicate channels.
After — every checkpoint
  • E1 sample, then undo the standardization to get back to data space.
  • E2 memorization, as a k-NN gap ratio on standardized tensors, against the untrained noise floor from D2.
  • E3 the same descriptor as B10b, on generated cells. Features that stay put across mutants and through the window are the structural signal.
  • E4 the window itself: structure onset to memorization onset.

So B10b is not bookkeeping. It is the left-hand side of the only comparison the paper rests on, which is why it is worth looking at the data before spending the GPU.
