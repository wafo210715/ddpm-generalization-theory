B6 · the checkpoint that decided the ring radius

Where a Diversity Ring Is Defined

B6 asks one question of every unit in the city: put a ring around it, and does that ring hold enough to compute a mix at all? The answer set the ring radius for the whole pipeline, and on the wrong data it set it wrong.

The test, on one unit

Shannon entropy needs two places in two categories

Entropy over a ring is −Σ p·log p across the categories present. With one place there is a single category at p = 1 and the entropy is zero. With none it is undefined. Neither is a measurement of mixed use, but both get written as zero, so a channel can look calm and be empty. The checkpoint counts how often the ring is non-degenerate.

written as zeroEmpty ring No places. Entropy has nothing to sum, so the pilot wrote zero — the same value a genuinely uniform place would get.
written as zeroOne category A real zero: no mix. Indistinguishable downstream from the empty case above.
a measurementTwo or more Only here does the number mean what the indicator claims. B6 counts the share of units that reach this.

What the picture shows that the number does not

In the dense core the difference is total. The densest tile in the city holds 2 places in OpenStreetMap and 681 in Overture. At a 50 m ring that is 0.0 % of units defined against 60.0 %. The checkpoint was not measuring whether this city has mixed use; it was measuring whether anyone had mapped it.

But move off the core and both sources thin out fast, and this corrects something I said earlier. A like-for-like re-run on B6's original 138 tiles gave 57 % at 50 m on Overture, and I reported that the ring could come back in to 100 m. Those 138 tiles are the ones OpenStreetMap had mapped densely — the central, well-surveyed part of the city — so they are a biased sample of the dataset, not a representative one. Across the 3 020 tiles that actually hold a hundred buildings or more, the inner-urban tile above has 26 places and the outer-urban tile has 2. The ring radius is still not settled, and it still depends on which tiles the dataset is made of — which is what B6 said in the first place.

Widening the ring fixes the number, and that is the trap. Watch the green grow in the 300 m column: it arrives as unions of discs around a few clusters. A unit counts as defined because it is near a mapped cluster, so the channel is largely reporting distance to the nearest cluster — which the accessibility channel already measures. The pilot's 0.88 correlation between diversity and accessibility has its mechanism right there in the picture.

So what the checkpoint now supports is narrower than "Overture fixes it". It fixes the core outright. Elsewhere the honest options are unchanged: a wider ring and an explicit note that the channel then encodes proximity to clusters, a plain count instead of entropy, restricting the dataset to tiles where the channel is defined, or treating diversity as a cell condition rather than a grid.

Settled by A1, later on 15 Sep. The test was run on all 3 020 tiles with a hundred buildings or more (scripts/report_ring_coverage_full.py): the ring is non-degenerate on 6.3 % of units at 50 m, 13.4 % at 100 m, 35.3 % at 300 m and 51.9 % at 500 m. On the 426 tiles above 2 000 buildings the 300 m figure is 94 %; on the 788 tiles between 100 and 250 buildings it is 4 %. The ring is fixed at 300 m for both place grids, absence is written as a value with the count grid to disambiguate it, and the dense core goes on the C7 mutant list rather than becoming the dataset. The reasoning is in indicator_specification.md §3.2.
